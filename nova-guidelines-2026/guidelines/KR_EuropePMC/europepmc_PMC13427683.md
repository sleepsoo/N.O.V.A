# 2026 Korean expert recommendations for the use of thrombopoietin receptor agonists in severe aplastic anemia

## Abstract

The treatment landscape for severe aplastic anemia (SAA) has undergone a significant paradigm shift with the integration of thrombopoietin receptor agonists (TPO-RAs) into standard immunosuppressive therapy (IST). However, clinical practice in Korea is shaped by a distinct regulatory and therapeutic environment characterized by the exclusive availability of rabbit antithymocyte globulin (rabbit ATG) and restrictive reimbursement policies governing TPO-RA use. This expert consensus statement provides a systematic framework and age-stratified treatment algorithms tailored to Korea-specific constraints. For treatment-naïve patients with SAA who are not candidates for immediate hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, a combination of rabbit ATG-based IST and TPO-RA is recommended as the first-line strategy. While eltrombopag is supported by robust global data, the panel emphasized the clinical utility of romiplostim in the Korean setting, given its established evidence in rabbit ATG-based regimens and the limited approved dosing of eltrombopag in Korea relative to pivotal global trials. The consensus also provides practical guidance on TPO-RA initiation, retreatment, and switching in refractory or relapsed disease, while emphasizing careful evaluation of clonal evolution in patients with insufficient response or newly emerging hematologic abnormalities, regardless of the specific TPO-RA used. Ultimately, these recommendations aim to standardize SAA management in Korea, optimize patient outcomes, and support future policy refinements to better align domestic practices with evolving global evidence.

## Introduction

Aplastic anemia (AA) is a bone marrow failure disorder characterized by hypocellular marrow and pancytopenia. In adults, most AA cases are acquired and are believed to involve immune-mediated destruction of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs). Activated CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and Th1 cells induce apoptosis of HSPCs through the perforin–granzyme and Fas–Fas ligand pathways. Additionally, proinflammatory cytokines such as interferon-γ and tumor necrosis factor-α further suppress hematopoiesis, leading to sustained bone marrow failure.

Traditionally, AA treatment has centered on immunosuppressive therapy (IST) based on anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG), cyclosporine (CsA), and allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) in patients with suitable donors. Although these treatment strategies have significantly improved patient survival, several limitations remain, including incomplete treatment responses, relapse, and clonal evolution during long-term follow-up, and the presence of patient populations in whom HSCT is not feasible owing to advanced age, comorbidities, or donor unavailability. Recently, the introduction of thrombopoietin receptor agonists (TPO-RAs) has caused a significant shift in the treatment paradigm for AA. TPO-RAs were initially introduced as a therapeutic option to induce hematopoietic recovery in patients refractory to or who relapsed after conventional IST. Subsequently, evidence has demonstrated that their combination with IST can improve both response rates and the speed of response, establishing TPO-RAs as an important component of first-line treatment strategies for patients who are not candidates for HSCT. These changes have been reflected in recent international clinical guidelines and expert consensus recommendations. Consequently, in some regions, a decreasing trend in the use of HSCT for AA has been observed.

## Purpose of the recommendation

In Korea, distinct clinical and regulatory constraints pose unique challenges to the management of AA. Horse ATG, which forms the backbone of many pivotal international studies, is unavailable in Korea; therefore, IST is delivered exclusively using rabbit ATG. In addition, based on pharmacokinetic profiles observed in East Asian populations, eltrombopag was approved at a lower maximum dose in Korea (75 in the first-line setting and 100 mg in the second-line setting) than the doses used in key global trials. The reimbursement landscape further limits routine practice, as TPO-RA coverage is primarily permitted in relapsed or refractory settings, whereas its use in combination with IST as a first-line therapy remains non-reimbursed. Together, these factors limit the direct application of international evidence to Korean practices. Nevertheless, TPO-RAs have become an integral part of modern AA treatment.

Accordingly, this expert consensus statement presented a practical framework and age-stratified treatment algorithms tailored to Korea-specific constraints. By recognizing TPO-RA plus IST as an important first-line treatment option despite current policy limitations, these recommendations aim to optimize patient outcomes and guide future policy refinements.

## Global positioning of TPO-RAs in the treatment paradigm for AA

The clinical role of TPO-RAs in AA has evolved stepwise over the past decade, in parallel with the accumulation of evidence from clinical trials. Initially, TPO-RAs were evaluated as second-line treatment options for patients who were refractory to or relapsed after conventional IST. Subsequently, their scope of application was expanded to include first-line treatments.

### Eltrombopag

Eltrombopag is a TPO-RA for which the clinical evidence of AA was first established, and its efficacy was initially demonstrated in the second-line treatment of patients who were refractory to or had relapsed after IST. In one study, eltrombopag monotherapy achieved a hematologic response rate of approximately 44% at 12 weeks, and 6 patients eventually met the response criteria in three lineages of the 11 responders who continued to receive eltrombopag at 150 mg per day for a median of 16 months. Subsequently, an extension study reported an overall response rate of approximately 40% at 3–4 months, with trilineage responses observed in 15%–20% of patients during a median treatment duration of 12 months.

In subsequent studies involving treatment-naïve patients, the addition of eltrombopag to standard IST (ATG plus CsA) significantly improved treatment outcomes. In a prospective study, eltrombopag was initiated at diagnosis and administered for six months, yielding an overall response rate (ORR) of 87% and a complete response (CR) rate of 39% at 6 months. The median time to response was approximately 3 months, which was markedly shorter than the 8–9 months typically observed after IST monotherapy. These findings were confirmed in a randomized controlled trial. In this study, the eltrombopag combination arm achieved a 6-month ORR of 68% and a CR rate of 30%, which were significantly superior to the outcomes of the IST monotherapy arm, which showed an ORR of 41% and a CR rate of 20%. However, most of these pivotal studies were conducted using horse ATG and permitted eltrombopag doses of up to 150 mg. Accordingly, caution is warranted when applying these findings to Korean clinical practice, where only rabbit ATG is available, and eltrombopag is approved at a maximum dose of 75 in the first-line setting and 100 mg in the second-line setting.

### Romiplostim

Romiplostim was introduced later than eltrombopag; however, it demonstrated strong efficacy, high response rates, and sustained hematopoietic recovery in both IST-refractory/relapsed patients and as first-line treatment. In early phase 2 studies involving patients with IST-refractory or relapsed AA, weekly administration of romiplostim at doses of 10–20 μg/kg resulted in an overall hematologic response rate of approximately 80% at week 27, with trilineage response rates of around 30%. These findings were subsequently expanded and confirmed in a multicenter phase 2/3 study. In that study, romiplostim was administered for up to 52 weeks, yielding an overall response rate of 84% at week 27, with responses maintained in 81% of the patients at week 53 and trilineage hematopoietic recovery in 39% of the patients. Collectively, these results suggest that long-term romiplostim treatment can induce durable hematopoietic recovery without loss of response.

The efficacy of romiplostim has also been clearly demonstrated in the first-line treatment of treatment-naive patients. In a single phase 2/3 study evaluating the combination of romiplostim with rabbit ATG-based IST, romiplostim was administered once weekly at doses of 10–20 μg/kg, with treatment extended up to 52 weeks in responders after an initial 6 months. As a result, the overall response rate at week 27 was 76.5%, with a complete response rate of 35.3%, markedly exceeding the 6-month response rates with rabbit ATG + CsA, which range from 33% to 57%. In addition, hematologic responses were observed within 14 weeks of treatment initiation in 41% of patients, suggesting that romiplostim combination therapy offers competitive efficacy in terms of response speed.

Notably, pivotal clinical studies on romiplostim predominantly enrolled East Asian patients, including those from Korea and Japan, and were conducted using rabbit ATG-based regimens, making the evidence particularly relevant to real-world clinical practice in Korea. Furthermore, as the maximum romiplostim dose used in these clinical studies (up to 20 μg/kg) is consistent with its approved dosing range in Korea, the efficacy demonstrated in clinical trials is more likely to be reproducible in routine practice compared with that using eltrombopag, lending further support to the practical applicability of romiplostim in the Korean clinical setting.

### Special considerations to select TPO-RAs in the Korean healthcare system

As outlined above, the Korean healthcare environment differs substantially from settings in which key clinical trials have been conducted. Accordingly, the selection of TPO-RAs for AA in Korea must consider local regulatory and reimbursement constraints, including limitations on drug availability, approved dosing, and insurance coverage. Notably, TPO-RA plus IST has already been adopted as a first-line treatment strategy for AA in other Asian countries, including Japan. According to a recent symposium on the current treatment landscape in Taiwan, TPO-RA plus IST is a first-line treatment option. Considering these international trends and the accumulating clinical evidence, we strongly recommend the use of TPO-RA combination therapy as the first-line treatment in Korea and emphasize that appropriate policy and reimbursement reforms are essential to support its implementation.

### Rabbit ATG

The fact that only rabbit ATG is available in Korea has important implications for the overall treatment strategies for AA. According to prospective randomized studies conducted by the U.S. National Institutes of Health (NIH) and meta-analyses by the European Society for Blood and Marrow Transplantation (EBMT), compared with rabbit ATG, horse ATG was associated with significantly higher response rates and superior survival at 3 and 6 months when used as the first-line IST. In contrast, a single-center retrospective analysis from Korea reported comparable response rates at 12 and 18 months and similar long-term survival outcomes between rabbit ATG-based IST and horse ATG-based IST, suggesting that a consistent and clear difference between rabbit and horse ATG was not observed. Nevertheless, even in this analysis, the time required to achieve CR was significantly longer in the rabbit ATG group than in the horse ATG group, a finding that was postulated to be related to the deeper and more prolonged lymphocyte depletion observed following rabbit ATG administration. Indeed, rabbit ATG exerts stronger immunosuppressive effects and has a longer persistence in vivo than does horse ATG, thereby requiring closer monitoring for infections and immunosuppression-related complications. These pharmacological and clinical differences are highly relevant when interpreting TPO-RA-combined IST in Korea, where rabbit ATG, rather than horse ATG, forms the backbone of the treatment. Accordingly, the efficacy and safety outcomes observed in Korea should not be directly compared to those reported in international trials conducted largely with horse ATG, and additional evidence in rabbit ATG-based settings is needed. This is particularly important when comparing eltrombopag and romiplostim in first-line settings.

Although the use of eltrombopag combined with IST is supported by robust phase 3 evidence, pivotal data have been derived largely from studies using horse ATG. In contrast, romiplostim, although supported by limited phase 3 comparative evidence, has been evaluated primarily in combination with rabbit ATG. Therefore, in Korea, treatment decisions regarding TPO-RA-combined IST should not be based solely on the apparent hierarchy of evidence but should also consider the ATG backbone and the clinical context in which the evidence was generated.

### PK/PD characteristics of eltrombopag in East Asian populations

In East Asian populations, including those from Korea, China, Japan, and Taiwan, the clearance of eltrombopag has been reported to be approximately 33% lower than that observed in Western populations. In a pharmacokinetic study involving healthy Japanese male volunteers, the systemic exposure, measured as the area under the concentration–time curve, to eltrombopag was nearly twice that in non-Japanese subjects. In consideration of these pharmacokinetic differences, several clinical studies of eltrombopag have set the initial dose for Asian patients with AA at approximately half of that used in other ethnic groups, with the study protocol specifying a 50% dose reduction for Asian patients. However, no direct comparative analyses have been conducted to determine whether this dose reduction strategy affects treatment responses in Asian patients compared to Western patients who received the full dose. Moreover, preliminary Korean real-world data presented at a recent scientific meeting suggested higher response rates to adequately dosed romiplostim than to limited-dose eltrombopag.

Taken together, these observations indicate that further evaluation is required to clarify how dose reduction strategies for eltrombopag in Asian patients influence hematopoietic recovery and overall treatment outcomes in AA.

### Korean reimbursement system

The Korean reimbursement system represents a major constraint in the real-world clinical application of TPO-RAs in Korea. Although both eltrombopag and romiplostim are approved for the treatment of AA, reimbursement coverage for their use in combination with IST as first-line treatment remains limited. These national reimbursement constraints directly influence treatment decisions in the initial stages, particularly in the selection of the TPO-RA plus IST strategy. Even for TPO-RA monotherapy in patients with relapsed or refractory AA, the Korean reimbursement criteria stipulate discontinuation after a maximum of six months of treatment. Retreatment was permitted only in patients who achieved a response during a 6-month treatment cycle and subsequently met the criteria for SAA. This policy differs substantially from treatment strategies employed in clinical trials.

In most pivotal-line treatment studies involving patients with relapsed or refractory AA, an initial response assessment was performed at 3–6 months, after which TPO-RAs were continued in responders without being limited to a 6-month treatment period. In a study by Olnes et al. (2012), the median duration of eltrombopag treatment was 16 months (range, 8–32 months), whereas in a study by Desmond et al. (2014), the median treatment duration was 12 months (range, 6–37 months), indicating that long-term administration exceeding 6 months is common. Similarly, the median treatment duration for romiplostim was 53 weeks (range, 35–155 weeks) in a study by Lee et al. (2019), and another study (Jang et al., 2021) reported continuous treatment for approximately 52 weeks, followed by observation. Taken together, these clinical data do not provide sufficient evidence to support the uniform discontinuation of TPO-RA monotherapy after 6 months when used as second-line treatment. Nevertheless, in real-world practice in Korea, clinicians are compelled to follow the current reimbursement regulations, which mandate treatment discontinuation after six months, followed by interruption and potential re-initiation of therapy.

In light of the above, TPO-RA-based treatment strategies in Korea should be designed with explicit consideration of domestic reimbursement constraints. This requires not only careful decisions regarding non-reimbursed drug use in the first-line setting, but also proactive planning for treatment interruption and retreatment in the second-line setting to optimize the overall treatment pathway.

## Recommended treatment algorithms incorporating TPO-RAs in Korea

- The treatment algorithm proposed in this recommendation focuses on the rational application of pharmacological treatment strategies that incorporate TPO-RAs in patients with SAA within the Korean healthcare environment.
- The algorithm is structured around three key factors: patient age, availability of a human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-matched sibling donor (MSD), and the use of TPO-RAs within the framework of domestic regulatory approval and reimbursement policies.
- This recommendation was not intended to guide HSCT. Therefore, this algorithm does not address decisions regarding HSCT from alternative donors compared with TPO-RA-based therapies.

### General principles of the Korean-Specific algorithm

1. In Korea, rabbit ATG combined with CsA is routinely used as the backbone of IST.
2. In the first-line treatment, TPO-RA therapy in combination with IST should be actively considered, and TPO-RA monotherapy as a second-line treatment should also be actively utilized as a viable clinical option.
3. The selection of a TPO-RA should be guided by a comprehensive assessment of international clinical evidence and the realities of the domestic reimbursement system.
 1. Eltrombopag has strong first-line evidence in a randomized phase 3 trial based on horse ATG. However, in Korea, only rabbit ATG is available, and there are regulatory dose limits (a maximum of 75 in the first-line setting and 100 mg in the second-line setting).
 2. In contrast, the evidence for the efficacy of romiplostim has been generated primarily from single-arm studies. Nevertheless, when outcomes from phase 3 clinical trials that included Korean and Japanese patients were indirectly compared with those of earlier eltrombopag studies, romiplostim demonstrated comparable or relatively favorable response rates for both first-line treatment in combination with IST and second-line monotherapy. Furthermore, its efficacy and safety have been evaluated in rabbit ATG-based settings, and clinically effective doses can be administered within the approved range in Korea, which may provide practical advantages.
4. After treatment initiation with IST-based therapy, the 6-month time point and the period thereafter should be regarded as key timeframes for treatment response assessment. This approach considers the delayed onset of responses with rabbit ATG-based IST and the fact that 6-month outcomes of IST-based therapy serve as a critical criterion for selecting TPO-RA as the second-line treatment under the domestic reimbursement system.
5. In cases of nonresponse or loss of response to IST-based therapy, the initiation or switching of TPO-RAs may be considered, as shown in Fig. 1. HSCT may also be considered, depending on the patient’s clinical condition.

### Patient categorization

In this algorithm, patients were categorized into three age groups: young, middle-aged, and older. This classification is not based on strict chronological age thresholds but rather represents a clinical stratification that comprehensively considers the feasibility of HSCT, the age distribution of patients enrolled in key studies of TPO-RA combination therapy, and the risks of infection and treatment-related complications.

### Young patients

- This group generally refers to patients younger than 40 years of age.
- If an MSD is identified, MSD-HSCT should be considered the preferred first-line treatment.
- In the absence of an MSD, the first-line treatment consists of IST based on rabbit ATG and CsA, with active recommendations for the TPO-RA combination.
- Hematological response should be assessed 6 months after the initiation of IST-based therapy.
 - ▪ If a of response:
 - ◦ For patients who do not receive TPO-RA as part of the first-line therapy, initiation of TPO-RA treatment may be considered.
 - ◦ For patients who received a TPO-RA in the first line:
 - ✓ If an initial response is achieved, retreatment with the same TPO-RA or switching to an alternative TPO-RA may be considered if the patient later relapses and fulfills the diagnostic criteria for SAA.
 - ✓ If no response is achieved via the first-line treatment, switching to an alternative TPO-RA may be considered.
 - ✓ When switching TPO-RAs, the transition from eltrombopag to romiplostim may be a practical option in Korea owing to the dose limitations of eltrombopag
- Consideration may also be given to HSCT from unrelated or haploidentical donors.

### Middle-aged patients

- Previous Korean guidelines have stratified first-line treatment strategies using an age cutoff of 50 years, recommending HSCT as the preferred option for patients < 50 years with an HLA-MSD, and IST as the standard treatment for patients aged ≥ 50 years, regardless of donor availability.
- However, with the significant improvement in outcomes achieved by TPO-RA plus IST, there is now a need to reconsider whether HSCT should be prioritized over TPO-RA plus IST as a first-line treatment in patients < 50 years. At the same time, advances in the safety and outcomes of transplantation have also highlighted the limitations of a strategy that uniformly excludes transplantation solely based on age ≥ 50 years.
- Accordingly, treatment strategies should not be dichotomized based on chronological age, but rather determined through a comprehensive assessment that integrates age with overall performance status, comorbidities, likelihood of treatment response, donor availability, and patient preferences regarding treatment options.
- According to this recommendation, adult patients with SAA aged 40–55 years, the predominant age range in major TPO-RA studies, were defined as the middle-aged patient group. For this group, treatment decisions should be guided by an individualized, multidisciplinary assessment rather than chronological age alone.
- For patients with HLA-MSDs, both MSD-HSCT and combination therapy with rabbit ATG, CsA, and TPO-RA may be considered with individualized decision-making. In the absence of an MSD, the first-line treatment should consist of IST based on rabbit ATG and CsA, with active recommendation for the TPO-RA combination.
- Hematologic response should be assessed 6 months after treatment initiation with IST-based therapy, and strategies for TPO-RA initiation, retreatment, switching, or transplantation in cases of non-response or loss of response should follow the same principles applied to the young patient group.

### Older patients

- Older patients generally include those aged 55–60 years or older in whom pharmacologic therapy is prioritized over transplantation.
- In this age group, first-line treatment with IST consisting of rabbit ATG and CsA combined with TPO-RA is recommended, and close monitoring of infections and immunosuppression-related complications is essential.
- Hematologic response should be assessed 6 months after treatment initiation, and strategies for TPO-RA initiation, retreatment, and switching should follow the same principles applied to young and middle-aged patient groups in cases of non-response or loss of response.
- HSCT should not be excluded solely based on chronological age; rather, it should be considered cautiously and stepwise after a comprehensive evaluation of comorbidities and overall medical fitness.
- Considerations of age in the use of rabbit ATG
 - ▪ A recent large retrospective analysis conducted by the NIH evaluated the efficacy and safety of IST combining ATG (horse ATG 82%, rabbit ATG 18%) with CsA ± eltrombopag in patients with SAA. In this study, outcomes in older patients aged ≥ 60 years (median, 68 years; range, 60–82 years) were compared to those in patients aged 18–59 years. No significant differences were observed between the two groups with respect to the major safety endpoints. Although overall survival was lower in the older group, this was largely attributable to poorer outcomes among non-responders and was not associated with an increased incidence of IST-related complications.
 - ▪ However, in Korea, where only rabbit ATG is currently available, caution is required when treating older patients with ATG. Horse and rabbit ATG differ substantially in their pharmacokinetic profiles and immunosuppressive effects in vivo. Compared to horse ATG, rabbit ATG has a much longer half-life (approximately 29.8 days vs. 5.7 days ), prolonged persistence in circulation, and more sustained cytokine-release induction and T-cell binding. These differences are also reflected in immune cell depletion patterns, with rabbit ATG being associated with deeper and more prolonged absolute lymphocyte depletion, including CD4+ T cells.
 - ▪ Accordingly, when rabbit ATG-based IST is administered to older patients with SAA, the prolonged immunosuppressive effects may increase the risk of opportunistic infections. Therefore, a more conservative approach that accounts for the pharmacokinetic characteristics of rabbit ATG, along with intensified monitoring strategies, is required for this population.

## Safety considerations

One of the most important safety concerns associated with the use of TPO-RAs is the risk of clonal evolution. In patients with AA, clonal evolution can occur as part of the natural disease course in approximately 10%–15% of cases, with a higher risk reported in treatment-refractory patients and those with short telomere length.

In clinical studies evaluating eltrombopag in patients with AA, clonal evolution was observed in approximately 8%–19% of patients, depending on the study. A substantial proportion of these cases is associated with chromosome 7 abnormalities (monosomy 7 or 7q abnormalities), and progression to myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) has been reported in some patients. In contrast, studies involving romiplostim generally reported a lower incidence of clonal evolution than did those involving eltrombopag. In several studies, no cytogenetic abnormalities or cases of MDS/acute myeloid leukemia (AML) have been observed, whereas other reports have described incidence rates typically ranging from 0% to 12.5%.

In clinical trials evaluating TPO-RAs in combination with IST, the reported incidence of clonal evolution and secondary hematological malignancies was comparable to that observed with IST monotherapy. Furthermore, both the frequency and patterns of clonal evolution have been reported to vary according to ethnicity and geographic region, suggesting limitations in attributing these risks solely to TPO-RA combination therapy. Therefore, in the management of patients with AA, insufficient treatment response or the emergence of new hematologic abnormalities should prompt a thorough evaluation for possible clonal evolution, including follow-up bone marrow examination and cytogenetic analysis. Such assessments should be performed consistently regardless of TPO-RA use or the specific agent selected.

## Summary and future perspectives

This expert consensus aims to systematically outline pharmacological treatment strategies, including TPO-RAs, for patients with SAA in the Korean healthcare setting and to propose a treatment algorithm that is readily applicable in real-world clinical practice. The recommendations comprehensively reflect the Korean clinical context, including the exclusive use of rabbit ATG and the current limitations in the approved dosing and reimbursement of TPO-RAs.

In Korea, IST based on rabbit ATG and CsA remains a central pillar of SAA treatment. Thus, TPO-RAs should be actively utilized as important strategic components to complement conventional therapies and optimize treatment responses. In patients eligible for HSCT, both transplantation and TPO-RA plus IST may be considered, whereas in patients who are unsuitable candidates for transplantation, a combination therapy with IST and TPO-RA is recommended. In cases of relapse or refractory disease after first-line therapy, TPO-RA monotherapy may be considered as a second-line option. Initiation, retreatment, or switching to an alternative TPO-RA may be considered based on the response to the TPO-RA used in the first-line setting.

Given the intrinsic disease characteristics of AA, the evaluation of clonal evolution, including bone marrow assessment and cytogenetic analysis, may be warranted during the disease course, regardless of TPO-RA use or the specific agent selected. Several unresolved issues remain, including the role and expected clinical benefits of TPO-RAs in patients with very severe aplastic anemia, reimbursement issues for transfusion-dependent patients without SAA, and the 6-month limit of TPO-RA treatment for second-line therapy under the current reimbursement system. In particular, in patients with very severe AA who are at high risk of infection and tend to show suboptimal responses to TPO-RA + IST, early consideration of HSCT, including alternative donor transplantation, may be warranted. These issues require further validation using real-world clinical data and should be continuously addressed in future revisions and refinements of the recommendations, as well as in ongoing clinical and policy discussions.
