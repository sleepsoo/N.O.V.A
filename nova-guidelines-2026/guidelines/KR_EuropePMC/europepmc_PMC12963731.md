# Diagnostic Implications of the Endocrine Society and American Academy of Pediatrics Guidelines on blood pressure: associations with sex, blood pressure components, and metabolic syndrome in Korean Adolescents

## Abstract

### Purpose

Pediatric hypertension and metabolic syndrome (MS) are increasing in parallel with childhood obesity. Variations in diagnostic thresholds between the Endocrine Society (ES) and American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) guidelines may affect detection and intervention timing.

### Methods

We analyzed data from 1,035 Korean adolescents aged 13–17 years drawn from the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (2014–2016). Blood pressure (BP) abnormalities and MS were assessed according to ES and AAP guidelines. Differences by sex, age, and body mass index (BMI) category were examined.

### Results

The ES guidelines identified significantly more cases of diastolic BP (DBP) abnormalities than the AAP guidelines. For example, ES identified DBP abnormalities in 15.2% in 16-year-old males versus 8.1% identified by AAP. This difference was especially prominent for prehypertensive categories. ES percentile-based thresholds were more sensitive to subtle diastolic elevations, while AAP uses fixed cutoffs that may underestimate early risk. MS prevalence exceeded 30% in multiple age groups among adolescents with BMI≥85th percentile. However, MS prevalence did not significantly differ between the 2 guidelines in any age or sex subgroup.

### Conclusions

Awareness of hypertensive status is essential in the era of increasing childhood and adolescent obesity. The ES guidelines might be more suitable for cardiometabolic screening in Korean adolescents than the AAP guidelines because they effectively detect hypertensive status, including DBP abnormalities.

## Highlights

· The Endocrine Society (ES) guideline identified diastolic blood pressure abnormalities more effectively than the American Academy of Pediatrics criteria.

· Prehypertension terminology and percentile-based thresholds enhance early risk detection in adolescents.

· Recognizing hypertensive status is essential amid rising adolescent obesity, and ES criteria may provide a more sensitive framework for timely cardiometabolic screening.

## Introduction

The developmental phase of childhood and adolescence is characterized by significant physical growth, particularly in height. This growth is positively correlated with blood pressure (BP), which has implications for cardiovascular health during these formative years. Globally, there has been a marked increase in the prevalence of obesity among children and adolescents, a trend that is closely linked to rising concerns about hypertension (HTN) in these age groups. The earlier stages of HTN, often referred to as pre-HTN (PreH) or elevated BP (EBP), are characterized by BP that is higher than normal but not yet in the hypertensive range. Identifying and diagnosing PreH/EBP is critical for preventing progression to HTN and for enabling early interventions to manage childhood obesity.

In response to these health challenges, 2 important clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) were published in 2017 by the Endocrine Society (ES) and the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP). These CPGs were tailored to the management of pediatric BP, each CPG with its own aims. The ES guidelines, titled "Pediatric Obesity—Assessment, Treatment, and Prevention: An Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline," emphasize the role of obesity in pediatric HTN. This approach underlines the connection between increased body mass index (BMI) and EBP, highlighting the importance of addressing obesity in HTN management.

The AAP's "Clinical Practice Guidelines for Screening and Management of High Blood Pressure in Children and Adolescents" focus on high BP management in children, with notable updates of the revision of BP reference tables compared to the 2004 "Fourth Report on the Diagnosis, Evaluation, and Treatment of High Blood Pressure in Children and Adolescents," providing a more comprehensive framework for healthcare providers.

In this study, we compare estimates of the prevalences of HTN, PreH or EBP, and metabolic syndrome (MS) among Korean adolescents based on ES and AAP diagnostic criteria, and consider the necessary responses and implications for applying these varying criteria in the pediatric and adolescent population.

## Materials and methods

### 1. Subjects

Among the 20,380 individuals enrolled in the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES) from 2014 to 2016, among 1,336 eligible adolescents, 1,035 were included in the final analysis after excluding participants with missing BP or MS component data (Fig. 1).

### 2. Study design and definition

The data collection procedures of KNHANES have been described in detail previously. Percentiles and z-scores for height, weight and BMI were obtained according to the 2017 Korea Growth Standards and examined statistically. The z-score for waist circumference (WC) was obtained from the Korean pediatric and adolescent WC reference published in 2022. Subjects were classified as having hypertensive status with positive combined BP if either systolic BP (SBP) or diastolic BP (DBP) was elevated. If both SBP and DBP were abnormal, the participant was counted once under the more severe category (i.e., HTN). This operational definition ensured that each subject contributed only once to the hypertensive status group, prioritizing the highest risk category.

BP groups were categorized as normal, PreH-ES (by the ES guideline) or EBP-AAP (by the AAP guideline), as well as HTN according to each guideline, and the prevalences of PreH-ES/EBP-AAP and HTN were analyzed. The BP percentile cutoff values were derived from a 2019 Korean reference based on KNHANES data, which provides age, sex, and height-adjusted BP percentiles in Korean children and adolescents.

In the ES guidelines, BP of >90th percentile to <95th percentile or <120/80 is PreH-ES, BP ≥95th percentile to <99th percentile + 5 mmHg is stage 1 HTN, and BP ≥99th percentile + 5 mmHg is stage 2 HTN according to age, sex, and height. In the AAP guidelines, after 13 years of age, EBP-AAP is defined as >120/80 to 129/80 mmHg, Stage 1 HTN is 130/80 to 139/89 mmHg, and stage 2 HTN is BP ≥140/90 mmHg regardless of age, sex, and height (Table 1).

MS is diagnosed using modified National Cholesterol Education Program III criteria. (1) HTN: blood pressure: SBP or DBP ≥90th percentile for age, sex, and height; (2) WC ≥90th percentile for age, sex, and ethnicity; (3) triglycerides ≥110 mg/dL; (4) high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ≤40 mg/dL; (5) fasting glucose ≥110 mg/dL. If three or more criteria were met, the patient was diagnosed with MS.

### 3. Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean±standard deviation for clinical characteristics and blood test results. The LMS method was used to calculate the z-score. McNemar test was used for within-subject comparisons (ES vs. AAP), and chi-square tests were used for between-group comparisons. Trend analysis was performed to compare the prevalence of BP status by sex and age, according to ES and AAP guidelines. The independent t-test was used to compare continuous variables between groups for the analysis of MS. Data were analyzed by IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 23.0 (IBM Co., USA).

### 4. Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Konkuk University Medical Center (IRB No. 2019-12-019).

## Results

The average z-scores for height, weight, and BMI across all age groups for both male and female subjects were 0–0.3 (2017 Korea Growth Standards), and the average z-score for WC was 0.1–0.3 (2022 Korean Pediatric and Adolescent WC reference values) (Table 2).

The study included 419 males and 380 females in the BMI<85th P group, and 129 males and 107 females in the BMI≥85th P group. The prevalences of BP abnormalities according to sex and diagnostic guidelines are presented in Fig. 2. For hypertensive status, the prevalence was higher in males than in females when assessed by both the ES (PreH-ES) and AAP guidelines (EBP-AAP and HTN-AAP). A similar pattern was observed for SBP abnormalities: males exhibited a higher prevalence than females in the PreH-ES, EBP-AAP, and HTN-AAP categories. In contrast, for DBP abnormalities, there were no significant differences between males and females in any category. However, within the same sex, the prevalence of PreH-ES was significantly higher than that of EBP-AAP in both males and females, indicating that the ES guidelines identified more cases of diastolic PreH-ES compared to the AAP guideline.

Figs. 3 and 4 compare the prevalences of BP abnormalities among Korean adolescents aged 13–17 years according to the ES and AAP guidelines, and in males and females, respectively. For both sexes, there were no significant differences between the ES and AAP guidelines in the overall prevalence of hypertensive status or SBP abnormalities alone. However, the prevalence of DBP abnormalities (PreH-ES vs. EBP-AAP) was significantly higher when using the ES guidelines. In males, significant differences were observed at ages 13, 16, and 17 (P<0.05), and in females at age 16 (P=0.043).

Table 3 presents the prevalences of BMI≥85th percentile, hypertensive status, and MS by age, sex, and diagnostic guideline among Korean adolescents aged 13–17 years. The prevalence of BMI≥85th percentile was consistently higher in males than females at ages 13–16 years, but the trend was reversed at age 17, when females (28.4%) surpassed males (23.2%). For hypertensive status, the prevalence was significantly higher in males than females across all age groups within each guideline (P<0.05). However, there were no statistically significant differences between the ES and AAP guidelines when comparing hypertensive status within the same sex and age group. MS prevalence among adolescents with BMI≥85th percentile, as defined by the ES and AAP guidelines, did not significantly differ between the 2 diagnostic criteria in any age or sex group. The sex-specific patterns revealed that males showed consistently high MS prevalence across all ages, whereas females exhibited greater variability, with higher rates observed during midadolescence (15–16 years).

## Discussion

In this study, we investigated the prevalences of BP abnormalities and MS among Korean adolescents aged 13–17 years, focusing on sex differences and the impacts of diagnostic guidelines (ES vs. AAP). We also examined MS prevalence in adolescents with BMI≥85th percentile, a group at elevated cardiometabolic risk.

Our findings demonstrate that the ES guidelines identified a significantly higher prevalence of DBP abnormalities, particularly PreH-ES, compared to the AAP guidelines. This difference was consistent across both sexes and most pronounced in males. The ES guidelines use percentile-based criteria that appeared more sensitive to subtle, age- and growth-related changes in DBP, while the fixed thresholds of the AAP guidelines may underestimate early diastolic abnormalities. These results suggest that percentile-based criteria may more accurately reflect underlying vascular risk in adolescents with evolving physiology.

The data demonstrated a notable dip in hypertensive status prevalence at age 14 across both sexes and guidelines. This decline may be attributable to transitional physiological processes during early adolescence, wherein pubertal growth spurts and rapid increases in height velocity induce transient fluctuations in BP. Accordingly, findings observed during this developmental period should be interpreted with caution.

The terminology used to describe BP categories also plays a critical role in clinical perception, public awareness, and health promotion. PreH-ES in the ES guidelines emphasizes early risk and underscores the need for intervention, whereas the AAP transition to EBP-AAP aims to reduce anxiety and avoid overtreatment but may unintentionally diminish urgency for early cardiovascular risk management. Given the rapid and steep rise in prevalence of childhood obesity and its associated cardiometabolic risks, terminology that communicates urgency, such as PreH-ES, may be more suitable for adolescents, for whom early intervention is crucial.

In Figs. 3A and 4A, the prevalence of hypertensive status increased steadily from ages 13 to 17, with a transition point where the proportion of HTN exceeded that of PreH-ES/EBP-AAP, at age 16 in males and age 14 in females. This pattern suggests that adolescents initially classified as PreH-ES or EBP-AAP may progress to overt HTN with age, reflecting the dynamic nature of BP during puberty. Notably, this progression to HTN appeared more pronounced under the AAP guidelines, which may be explained by DBP thresholds that fail to identify early abnormalities as EBP, thereby resulting in a sharper apparent increase in HTN prevalence.

The term “prehypertension” has the advantage of conveying a clear message that an adolescent is on a trajectory toward HTN, thereby encouraging timely preventive efforts. It reflects the continuum of cardiovascular risk and is particularly useful for guiding early intervention strategies in youth. However, it may also lead to overmedicalization and psychological distress in some individuals. In contrast, the term “elevated blood pressure” was adopted by AAP to reduce such concerns, but may weaken the perceived urgency of early risk. Given our findings, the use of " prehypertension" appears to align more effectively with the need for early risk recognition in adolescents with elevated BMI or central obesity.

To visually reflect these conceptual differences, Fig. 3 (males) and Fig. 4 (females) apply distinct charting formats according to the diagnostic guidelines. For the ES guidelines, stacked bar charts were used to emphasize the cumulative burden of BP abnormalities, with PreHES and HTN displayed together to illustrate the continuum of cardiovascular risk. The cumulative values of PreH-ES and HTN are labeled above the stacked bars, further clarifying the total hypertensive burden with the ES definition. In contrast, AAP data were presented as grouped bar charts, consistent with a categorical approach that separates EBP-AAP from HTN. This design underscores the AAP focus on distinguishing categories rather than highlighting progression. Together, these visualization strategies not only enhance clarity but also reinforce the differing diagnostic philosophies of the ES and AAP guidelines, ultimately shaping clinical interpretation and public health messaging in adolescent BP screening.

DBP is more closely associated with increased adiposity in children and adolescents than SBP, and is particularly sensitive to excess weight and central obesity. In our correlation analysis (Table 4), significant associations between WC z-scores and DBP abnormalities were observed only at age 16, in both males (ES: r=0.34, P=0.0006; AAP: r=0.34, P=0.0006) and females (ES: r=0.21, P=0.032; AAP: r=0.25, P=0.012). At other ages, correlations were weaker and not statistically significant. This pattern suggests that midadolescence may represent a critical period when central adiposity exerts a stronger influence on DBP. From the perspective of clinical obesity, more sensitive detection of DBP abnormalities during childhood and adolescence is meaningful for the management of obesity, as it enables earlier identification and targeted intervention for at-risk youth. In the context of rising childhood and adolescent obesity in South Korea, heightened clinical attention to DBP, especially when using percentile-based criteria like those of the ES guideline, may be critical for the timely identification of high-risk individuals. More sensitive detection of DBP abnormalities during adolescence may facilitate early preventive strategies and improve long-term outcomes.

No significant differences in hypertensive status or SBP prevalence were observed between the 2 guidelines across age groups. However, BP abnormalities were consistently more prevalent and appeared earlier in males, consistent with previous studies suggesting that pubertal timing and adiposity patterns contribute to earlier cardiovascular risk in boys.

Analysis of MS revealed that overall prevalence did not differ meaningfully between the ES and AAP definitions, indicating that BP guideline choice has limited impact on MS classification. This suggests that other components, such as central obesity, dyslipidemia, and impaired glucose metabolism, may drive MS diagnosis more strongly in adolescents. MS prevalence was higher among males at age 13, but this sex difference diminished with age. In males, MS prevalence remained high throughout adolescence, while in females it increased with age, peaking at 16 years. These trends may reflect sex-specific physiological differences and highlight the need for tailored screening strategies.

Among adolescents with BMI≥85th percentile, MS prevalence was high in both sexes, exceeding 30%–40% in several age groups. Notably, MS prevalence in this subgroup did not significantly differ between the 2 BP diagnostic guidelines, reinforcing the robustness of MS classification and suggesting that BP criteria choice does not substantially affect MS diagnosis in adolescents at elevated obesity risk.

A key strength of this study is the use of nationally representative KNHANES data, which ensures generalizability to the broader Korean adolescent population. Direct comparisons of the ES and AAP guidelines provide practical insight into how guideline selection influences identification of at-risk youth. Stratified analyses by sex, age, and BMI percentile further refine understanding of metabolic and vascular risk during adolescence.

Several limitations should be noted. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference or longitudinal risk assessment. Sample size limitations in certain subgroups may have reduced statistical power. Pubertal stage was not directly assessed, which could influence BP and metabolic parameters. Finally, MS was defined using surrogate measures, which may not fully capture the complexity of cardiometabolic health.

In summary, the ES guidelines identified significantly more cases of DBP abnormalities than the AAP guidelines, supporting the use of age-, sex-, and height-specific percentiles in the context of increasing childhood and adolescent obesity and earlier pubertal onset. This nationally representative study also found a high prevalence of BP abnormalities and MS among Korean adolescents, especially those with BMI≥85th percentile. By integrating sensitive diagnostic criteria, urgency-communicating terminology, and cumulative visualizations, the ES approach underscores the importance of early intervention. These findings highlight the need for clear risk communication and proactive, guideline-based screening to reduce long-term cardiovascular risk in adolescents.

In conclusion, awareness of hypertensive status is essential in the era of increasing childhood and adolescent obesity. By emphasizing early intervention through its terminology and visualization, the ES guidelines highlight the importance of timely management. Therefore, ES criteria may be more appropriate for adolescent cardiometabolic screening in Korea.
