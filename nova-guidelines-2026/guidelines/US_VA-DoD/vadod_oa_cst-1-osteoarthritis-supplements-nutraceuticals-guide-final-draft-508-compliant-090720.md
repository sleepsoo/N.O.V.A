# **Osteoarthritis Supplements & Nutraceuticals Guide**

## Purpose

Over-the-counter dietary supplements and nutraceuticals are commonly used by people with osteoarthritis. This guide summarizes the evidence and recommendations presented in the 2026 VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guideline for the Non-Surgical Management of Hip and Knee Osteoarthritis.

Clinical studies often measure outcomes using standardized tools. Throughout this guide:

- **VAS** (Visual Analog Scale) measures pain intensity.

- **• WOMAC** (Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index) measures pain, stiffness, and physical function related to osteoarthritis.

## How to Use This Guide

The evidence summaries below reflect Appendix J of the VA/ DoD Clinical Practice Guideline. For most supplements, there is insufficient evidence to recommend for or against routine use. CBD oil is the exception and is not recommended for hip or knee osteoarthritis.

- Use shared decision-making to discuss the potential benefits, harms, evidence, and individual preferences.

- Continue guideline-supported osteoarthritis care. Some supplements may be considered in addition to, not as a replacement for, standard treatment.

- More research is needed to better understand the effectiveness, safety, dosing, and quality of these products.

## Things to Discuss with Your Healthcare Provider

When considering supplements or nutraceuticals:

- What symptoms or goals are you hoping to improve?

- Tell your healthcare provider about all supplements and medications you take.

- Discuss that supplement quality and ingredient consistency can vary.

- Remember that supplement quality and ingredients can vary between brands.

- “Natural” does not always mean safe or effective.

- Consider whether the benefits are worth the cost.

- Supplements should not replace proven treatments such as exercise, weight management, physical therapy, or prescribed medications.

- Ask if any supplements may interact with your medications or medical conditions.

Refer to the full guideline for a detailed review of the supporting evidence and research studies.

## Key Takeaway

Many supplements are marketed for osteoarthritis, but research on their benefits varies. They may be used alongside proven treatments, but they should not replace exercise, weight management, physical therapy, or prescribed medications.
