Sidebar 2: Contraindications to Systemic Menopausal
Estrogen Therapy
Absolute Contraindications:
• Active or recent arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., stroke and
MI)
• Active or recent DVT or PE
• Active or severe hepatic impairment or disease
• Known CVD and age > 60 years old and/or >10 years after
menopause onset
• Breast cancer or history of breast cancer, except in appropriately
selected patients being treated for metastatic diseases
• Estrogen-dependent neoplasia
• Known anaphylactic reaction or angioedema related to estrogen use
• Known or suspected pregnancy
• Protein C, protein S, or antithrombin deficiency, or other known
thrombophilic disorders
• Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding
Exercise caution for:
• Patients at risk of CVD, and utilize validated cardiovascular risk
calculator (PREVENTa, ASCVD, and/or VA Women CVD Risk
Scoreb)
• Patients on thyroid replacement therapy should have thyroid function
monitored
• Patients with gallbladder disease (as estrogens increase the risk of
gallbladder disease)
• Patients with history of DVT, PE, stroke, or MI
• The development of severe hypercalcemia, loss of vision, severe
hypertriglyceridemia or cholestatic jaundice (discontinue estrogen)
For patients with absolute contraindications or where caution should be
exercised, refer to the appropriate specialist(s) for individualized
counseling and shared decision-making.
Abbreviations: ASCVD: atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease; CVD:
cardiovascular disease; DVT: deep vein thrombosis; MI: myocardial
infarction; PE: pulmonary embolism; PREVENT: Predicting Risk of
Cardiovascular Disease EVENTs; VA: Veterans Affairs
