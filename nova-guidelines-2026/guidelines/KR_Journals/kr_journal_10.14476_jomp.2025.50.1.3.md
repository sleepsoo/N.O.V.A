## INTRODUCTION

Temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) encompass a range of conditions that affect the temporomandibular joint, surrounding musculature, and associated structures. These disorders frequently result in chronic pain, functional impairment, and diminished quality of life for affected individuals. Although advancements in research have led to significant improvements in understanding TMD pathophysiology, misconceptions and outdated treatment practices persist in certain regions, including Korea.

Historically, TMD was often attributed to malocclusion or mandibular misalignment, leading to the widespread application of irreversible occlusal adjustments and invasive interventions. However, contemporary research has demonstrated that TMD arises from a multifactorial interplay of musculoskeletal, neurological, and psychosocial influences. Consequently, treatment approaches have shifted toward non-invasive, evidence-based management strategies that prioritize the patients’ well-being and symptom relief.

In response to these evolving insights, the International Network for Orofacial Pain and Related Disorders Methodology (INfORM), in collaboration with the International Association for Dental Research (IADR), has established updated clinical practice guidelines for TMD management. These recommendations, finalized during the IADR General

Session in March 2024, aim to provide a global standard for TMD diagnosis and treatment.

Despite these advances, certain clinical settings in Korea continue to employ irreversible occlusal modifications for TMD management, necessitating greater awareness and implementation of the updated guidelines. This editorial highlights the core principles of the INfORM/IADR recommendations [1], their implications for Korean dental practice, and strategies to prevent the continued use of irreversible occlusal treatments.

## PREVENTING IRREVERSIBLE OCCLUSAL TREATMENT IN KOREA

Despite global efforts to standardize evidence-based TMD care, some Korean dental practitioners continue to employ irreversible occlusal adjustments as a primary treatment modality for TMD. These interventions, often performed under the assumption that occlusal discrepancies are the root cause of TMD, lack scientific support and pose risks of exacerbating patient symptoms.

The INfORM/IADR guidelines strongly advise against permanent occlusal modifications for TMD management, emphasizing the necessity of conservative and reversible treatments. To ensure that these principles are widely adopted in Korea, the following measures should be considered.

## CONCLUSION

The updated INfORM/IADR Key Points for Good Clinical Practice represent a significant advancement in the global standardization of TMD treatment, advocating for conservative, scientifically supported management strategies. However, for these guidelines to be effectively implemented in Korea, proactive efforts must be made to eliminate outdated and irreversible treatment approaches.

The continued use of permanent occlusal modifications for TMD management in Korea highlights the need for immediate educational and institutional reforms. Dental professionals, academic institutions, and regulatory organizations must collaborate to ensure that evidence-based practices are integrated into clinical settings.

By adopting these updated guidelines and transitioning away from irreversible occlusal interventions, the Korean dental community can improve patient outcomes, minimize treatment-related complications, and align with the latest advancements in TMD research.

Ensuring that all TMD patients in Korea receive scientifically validated, patient-centered care is not only an ethical obligation but also a necessary step toward the future of orofacial pain management.
